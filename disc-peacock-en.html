What is the I Peacock type?

The DISC behavioral style theory was introduced by psychologist William Marston in his 1928 book Emotions of Normal People. It divides behavioral style into four tendencies: D (Dominance), I (Influence), S (Steadiness) and C (Conscientiousness). Describing them as D Tiger, I Peacock, S Koala and C Owl is a popular way, added later by others, to make them easier to remember.

Everyone has all four tendencies, just in different proportions. "I Peacock type" means the I tendency stands out most. It does not mean the person has only the I Peacock in them.

Strengths and blind spots of the I Peacock

Strengths

  • Contagious energy: Their enthusiasm lifts everyone's spirits.
  • Good at expressing ideas: They can explain ideas in a lively, interesting way that people want to listen to.
  • Wide network: They easily build relationships with all kinds of people.
  • Optimistic and flexible: When things change, they quickly see new possibilities.

Blind spots

  • Plenty of ideas and quick starts, but follow-through and details are easily overlooked.
  • Sometimes say yes to too many things and run out of time.
  • Care about what others think, and can be affected when ignored or rejected.
  • May lean on feelings when making decisions and check facts less often.

What work environment suits the I Peacock?

Behavioral style does not decide what work you can do, but it does affect which environments energize you. The I Peacock is usually most comfortable where there is interaction with people, a relaxed atmosphere, room to express ideas, and results that are seen and appreciated. Examples include marketing and planning, sales, PR and events, teaching and training, and customer service.

On the other hand, working alone for long periods, doing a lot of repetitive paperwork, or often being ignored is what most easily drains motivation for an I Peacock.

How to communicate with an I Peacock

  • Start with a relaxed atmosphere, and give them room to speak.
  • Explain with stories or scenarios before numbers.
  • Confirm important details and deadlines again in writing afterward.
  • When you have concerns, acknowledge their ideas first, then raise them.

Under stress: An I Peacock under stress may become emotional, talk more, or grow impatient, and may put off things that need handling to avoid unpleasantness. This is usually a sign that they need to be understood.

FAQ

Is the I Peacock just a show-off?

No. The I Peacock's enthusiasm and expressiveness is how they connect with people and lift the mood, not a way to grab the spotlight.

Is a person only one animal?

No. Everyone has all four tendencies, and it is common for two to be clearly visible.

Is the DISC behavioral style test accurate?

The DISC behavioral style test describes your behavioral tendencies based on your own answers. It is useful for understanding yourself and improving communication, but it is not a score of your abilities or a diagnostic tool, and the result is for reference only.
